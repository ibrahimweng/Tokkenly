# Gradient set — palette and rules

High-key throughout. There is no black anywhere: the darkest note in the whole
set is `#123a5c`, and it is used sparingly.

## Ramps

Each ramp runs light to saturated. A shape takes one ramp, usually top-to-bottom,
sometimes diagonal.

## Rules

1. Completely flat. No cast shadow, no drop shadow, no dimensional edge.
2. No outlines. Shapes are told apart by colour alone.
3. One gradient per shape, light to saturated.
4. Gradients may cross hue — blue to green on the tag, pink to red on the stack.
5. Reduce the form hard. Two or three shapes is usually enough.
6. Colour serves harmony, not realism. The coffee cup is green.
7. One or two accent details, then stop.

## The one trap

A pale object has to stay *cool* against the warm ground, and a warm object has
to carry enough body to separate from it. `paper` ends in blue rather than white
for exactly this reason; the first pass used a warm near-white and the house,
calendar, receipt and carton all dissolved into the background.

A pale object still gets strong accents — that is what keeps it from washing
out. See `calendar`: pale body, coral header, saturated blue dots.

## Purple

There is none, deliberately. It is not in the reference.
